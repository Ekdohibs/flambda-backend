# C4, the heap: status

The machine runs the heap. `FlambdaSemanticsSpec.step` runs a `Let` of
a primitive by `FlambdaHeapSpec.exec_prim` on the heap of its state
(`exec_named`): a block with an identity is made, mutable or
immutable and unique; a field is written; a mutable array of values
is made, written, read and measured; and a load, `Get_tag`, `Is_int`,
`Is_null`, the length of an array and `Phys_equal` read a cell, or
otherwise answer of a location that the heap binds, where they are
given one. A state has six components: the heap and
a read record come after the static environment. The result of a
step that leaves the term carries both; what is observed of an exit
(`Exits`) is the continuation, the values and the heap, and not the
record. Every statement that was proved before this step is proved on
this machine, in its text with the heap and the record, or was
restated by its owner with a reviewer's word ("The machine runs a
`Let` on the heap", below). No statement was dropped.

  machine of regions at a `Let` that needs the heap was printed as
  not run. The third is gone since, with the limit that it printed

## The kinds of call

This is not of the heap; it is here because the work was mine.

The model's call has the compiler's three kinds
(`FlambdaModel.call_kind`): `Direct`, `Indirect_unknown_arity`, and
`Indirect_known_arity` with a list of code ids or none. A call of
known arity is defined only where it is a full call of code that is
not tupled and that its list allows, and it is then the call of
unknown arity of the same callee (decisions D58 and D59;
`semantics/FlambdaKnownAritySpec.v` has the statements, and the CR at
`FlambdaSemanticsSpec.shape_of_closure_call` asks the developers what
the kind promises). A direct call and a call of unknown arity are as
they were.

What the validation does with the new kind, on the term before:

- In the code of a stub, a direct call that is known to be full is
  made a call of known arity, as Simplify makes it (C3's
  `FlambdaStubCallsSpec.demote_full_stub_calls`, which every check of
  the validation now runs where it ran `demote_stub_calls`).
- A call of known arity of a symbol that the unit binds, outside
  code, to a closure is made a direct call
  (`FlambdaKnownSymbolsSpec.fold_known_apply`): Simplify has a direct
  call, or what it made of one, where it inlined the stub. The law of
  one call is one-way for this kind
  (`fold_known_apply_sound_statement`): a call of known arity is
  undefined more often than the direct call
  (`fold_known_apply_defines_statement` has one that the rewrite gives
  a step).

Limits. Each is of what the validation can match, so each can lose
a verdict; what a verdict that is given says is as before:

- **The validation has no promotion.** Simplify may mark a call of
  unknown arity as a call of known arity that lists code ids, where
  the type of its callee does not say which closure it is and does
  give the code ids that the callee may have, each of code whose
  metadata it has, that is not tupled and that has the arity of the
  arguments of the call (`simplify/simplify_apply_expr.ml`,
  `simplify_function_call`, by `T.meet_code_ids`). It was seen at
  `-O3`, where closures meet at a continuation and the call is of its
  parameter. The term before has a call of unknown arity there, and
  no rewrite of the validation makes the one of the other: a pair in
  which such a call is still there in every comparison that the
  validation makes loses its verdict. Units were seen to lose theirs
  so when the validation took the new kind; it is not shown that
  every unit with such a call does, and other rewrites can remove the
  call. What is meant to recover them: a rewrite of the term before
  that marks the call where the term itself shows that every
  application of the continuation gives the parameter a closure of
  code that passes the tests of D58. It is not written, and nothing
  is proved of it. It would not reach a parameter that goes into a
  value slot of a closure and is called in the code of that closure.
  The model's own Simplify from the types
  (`simplify/FlambdaSimplifyApplySpec.v`) does not make the mark
  either: it has no join of what the applications of a continuation
  give (`simplify/FlambdaSimplifyHandlersSpec.v`).
- The other rewrites that make a call of unknown arity direct leave
  a call of known arity, as their comments say
  (`FlambdaKnownClosuresSpec.call_known_closure`,
  `FlambdaClosureOfCodeSpec.call_code_closure`,
  `FlambdaClosureCallsSpec.call_made_closure`,
  `FlambdaAliasesSpec.canonical_call_kind`,
  `FlambdaSimplifyApplySpec.direct_call_kind`). `fold_known_apply`
  runs where what the unit binds its symbols to is used
  (`FlambdaValidateSpec.with_known`): in the comparisons of the terms
  in which something was lifted, and not in the normalisation of the
  comparisons before them (`normalise_before`). So a pair that needs
  it may need one of the comparisons of lifted terms, where the same
  pair with a call of unknown arity did not.

## The primitives, said of the cells that their arguments name

Added after the step above: five statements of `FlambdaHeapSpec.v`,
with five definitions that they use, proved in
`FlambdaHeapProofs.v`, for a machine whose heap has cells that it is
not to look at. No definition or statement that was there changes.
C2 asked for them, for the machine of regions with cells; C3 gave
its word on the last as the law that the erasure of regions needs.
The laws that there were ask a property of every value of the heap
(`exec_prim_preserves_statement`), or two heaps alike in every entry
(`exec_prim_related_statement`), and such a heap need not give
either. Each of the five but `cell_with_values_statement` is of an
execution that has a value (`exec_prim` gives `Some`).

- `exec_prim_preserves_given_statement`: a property holds of the
  value of a primitive that is not of the heap, where it holds of
  the arguments and of every value of the cells that the heap binds
  the locations among the arguments to (`heap_values_all_given`),
  under five of the hypotheses of `exec_prim_preserves_statement`
  on the property: it holds of every constant and of every boxed
  number, of a block or an immutable array exactly when of what it
  holds, and of what a static block or a static array holds when of
  its symbol. That it holds of every location, which that statement
  asks, is not asked here. A location inside an argument names no
  cell here. The primitives of the heap are left out; that is not
  said to be needed.
- `exec_prim_load_statement`: the value of a load from a block or
  from an array (`prim_loads_cell`) whose first argument is a
  location is a value that the cell of that location holds. No
  property is asked or given.
- `exec_prim_adds_statement`: a primitive of the heap has added one
  entry in front of the heap. An allocation binds the fresh location
  to a cell whose values are the arguments. A write binds the
  location that is its first argument to its cell with one value
  replaced by the last argument (`cell_with`; by
  `cell_with_values_statement` each value of the cell after is the
  new one or a value of the cell before).
- `exec_prim_related_given_statement`: under the hypotheses of
  `exec_prim_related_statement` on the relation of values and on
  the two static environments, what a primitive does on some
  arguments and a heap it does on related arguments and another
  heap, where the two heaps bind the same locations, entry for
  entry, and the cells that the arguments name are alike
  (`heap_alike_given`). Each heap gains nothing, or an entry at one
  location with cells alike (`heap_alike_added`). It goes one way.

What they do not say. That two heaps bind the same locations is
asked of the whole of both heaps, not of the cells named: an
allocation takes its location from all of them. No premise is said
to be the weakest for a given primitive. `heap_alike_given` looks at
the entry that `lookup` finds, and at no entry that a later write
hides. `heap_alike_added` does not say which location the entry is
at: that the cell of a location bound before, which no argument
names, is found after as it was comes with
`exec_prim_adds_statement` and `exec_prim_heap_statement`.

`exec_prim_related_statement` keeps its text and is now proved from
`exec_prim_related_given_statement`: the section of lemmas that
proved the first proves the second. Four lemmas of that section
are restated under new names; no other file named them. No
extracted file changes.

Read, each in its scope and none as a review of the proofs: the five
statements and their comments as Spec text, before a proof was
placed, by R2 and R4; the ten names by N1; and by C2, C3 and C5,
each the ones that it asked for or means to use.

## Review

five blocking findings. The third version answers them; "What changed
since the second version" in the request says where. R2 has approved
the third version with its prototype, as a design and a prototype

## Decided overnight

The human is away and has said: "If there are considerations to be
made, document them, take a sensible decision and proceed; and
tomorrow morning we can review them." The orchestrator has taken the
twelve decisions of the request as recommended there, as amended by
R2's reviews, for the human to review. Each: the question; the
options; the choice; why; what it costs to change later. The numbers
are those of "Decisions for the human" in the request.

1. **What has an identity.** Options: only blocks made `Mutable` or
   `Immutable_unique`; every allocation; the first plus closures.
   Choice: the first. Why: the compiler shares and duplicates
   immutable blocks, boxed numbers and closures (CSE, lifting,
   unboxing), so a model that told them apart would refute rewrites
   the compiler makes. To change: every block of every statement goes
   in the heap; a redesign.
2. **What a location is.** Options: a name with a fresh one chosen by
   a function; the next number. Choice: a name. Why: D29, and a
   bijection between runs is needed either way. To change: nothing in
   statements; `fresh_location` becomes transparent.
3. **`Phys_equal`.** Options: (a) no value on anything allocated; (b)
   defined but on two immutable allocated values; (c) (b) and false
   by shape; (d) by contents; (e) an oracle. Choice: (b), on top of
   what P1 landed for immediates, which has no value on null or on
   poison either; the prototype follows it. Why: it gives `==` its
   meaning where something has an identity and on immediates; (d) is
   not preserved by Simplify (R2-21). It is weaker coverage: nothing
   is proved of a program after it compares two immutable allocated
   values. To change to (c): leaf work in the primitives' files. To
   (e): `state` and `refines` change, an epoch.
4. **A load marked `Immutable` from a block made `Mutable`.** Options:
   (a) no value; (b) field stability (the load makes the field
   stable, a store into a stable field has no value); (c) mutability
   by field from the compiler. Choice: (a) for epochs 1 to 3, then
   (b). Why: (a) is the simplest sound start and (b) is exactly what
   Simplify assumes, with no change to the compiler. (a) leaves
   records with both sorts of field undefined, so it must not outlast
   the first epochs. To change from (a) to (b): the heap's files only
   (a cell remembers stable fields; `no_effects` and `no_coeffects`
   restated; the relation orders the marks). Outside the model by the
   human's rulings: tail-modulo-cons, and `Duplicate_block` followed
   by stores. The conversion refuses `Duplicate_block`. It did not
   refuse the stores of tail-modulo-cons in a term read from the text
   that the compiler printed then: a run that came to one was
   undefined, and where the run of the term before came to one the
   validation said nothing of that run. It refuses them in a term
   read from a text that writes their mark, as the compiler's does
   since (the first item under "What this step does not do",
   above). Recursive values: X5
   found nothing wrong; they are calls of C functions, which the
   model does not have. Recursive modules (X5-2): outside what any
   stage covers, since a field may be read before the run-time
   library overwrites it.
5. **`Block_set` into what was not made `Mutable`.** Options: no
   value; write anyway. Choice: no value. Why: the front end makes no
   such store, and Simplify keeps what it knows of the block. To
   change: one case of `exec_prim`.
6. **What an exit observes.** Options, cumulative: (i) values and
   heap as they are; (ii) up to a bijection on locations; (iii)
   immutable values by contents, through symbols (D6); (iv) poison of
   the source is anything of its kind (D26). Choice: (i) for epoch 2,
   (ii) and (iii) in epoch 3, (iv) after. Why: each step is sound by
   itself and the proofs come before the epoch that needs them. To
   change: `refines` is restated, an epoch, with the lemma that
   carries the old theorems over.
7. **Closures at exit.** Options: the whole value; less, by a
   relation on code ids. Choice: the whole value (D1 as it is). To
   change: a third component of the correspondence, S4's.
8. **How the heap enters the statements.** Options: a fifth field of
   the state; one record for the static environment and the heap.
   Choice: a fifth field. Why: less to change now, and nothing
   planned adds to the memory. To change: a second sweep of the same
   size.
9. **`expr_refines` over closed memories only.** Options: yes, in the
   definition; no. Choice: yes. Why: the lemma on related states
   needs it and every state reached from a term is closed. To change:
   the premise moves to each theorem.
10. **Scope.** In: mutable and unique blocks of values on the heap.
    Not modelled: local allocation and regions (the conversion drops
    regions and reads a local allocation as one on the heap, D37).
    Out: atomics, bigarrays, lazy
    blocks, off-heap memory; static blocks that are not `Immutable`
    until symbols are addresses; `Duplicate_block` by ruling. Later,
